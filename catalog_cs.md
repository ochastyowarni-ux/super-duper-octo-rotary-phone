# University Department of Computer Science: Undergraduate Academic Catalog & Student Policy Handbook

## 1. Degree Requirements: Bachelor of Science in Computer Science (B.S. CS)

### 1.1 Total Credit Requirement
To earn a Bachelor of Science in Computer Science, a student must complete a minimum of **120 semester credit hours**, distributed as follows:
- **General Education Core**: 36 credit hours
- **Computer Science Major Core**: 48 credit hours
- **Computer Science Technical Electives**: 18 credit hours
- **Mathematics and Natural Sciences Support**: 18 credit hours

### 1.2 Minimum GPA Requirement for Graduation
Students must graduate with:
- A cumulative grade point average (CGPA) of at least **2.00** on a 4.00 scale across all university coursework.
- A major grade point average (Major GPA) of at least **2.25** in all courses designated as CS and technical support electives.
- No course with a grade lower than **C (2.0)** may count toward the Major Core requirements. A grade of C- is considered failing for core prerequisite advancement.

## 2. Computer Science Course Catalog and Prerequisites

### CS 101: Introduction to Computer Science and Programming (4 credits)
- **Description**: Fundamental concepts of structured programming, variables, control flow, functions, basic data structures (lists, dictionaries), and algorithmic problem-solving using Python.
- **Prerequisites**: Placement into MATH 115 (Pre-Calculus) or higher, or a score of >= 60 on the University Math Placement Exam.
- **Corequisites**: CS 101L (Laboratory Component).

### CS 102: Object-Oriented Programming and Software Development (4 credits)
- **Description**: Principles of object-oriented design, inheritance, polymorphism, abstract classes, interfaces, exception handling, unit testing, and GUI development in Java.
- **Prerequisites**: Minimum grade of C in CS 101 and MATH 115.

### CS 201: Data Structures and Algorithmic Analysis (4 credits)
- **Description**: Linear and non-linear data structures: dynamic arrays, linked lists, stacks, queues, trees, heaps, hash tables, and graphs. Time and space complexity analysis (Big-O notation).
- **Prerequisites**: Minimum grade of C in CS 102 and completion of MATH 151 (Calculus I).

### CS 202: Computer Organization and Assembly Language (3 credits)
- **Description**: Digital logic, register transfer logic, von Neumann architecture, instruction set architectures (RISC-V/x86), memory hierarchy, cache mechanisms, and assembly language programming.
- **Prerequisites**: Minimum grade of C in CS 102.

### CS 301: Design and Analysis of Algorithms (3 credits)
- **Description**: Advanced algorithm design strategies including divide-and-conquer, greedy algorithms, dynamic programming, network flow, and NP-completeness.
- **Prerequisites**: Minimum grade of C in CS 201 and MATH 210 (Discrete Mathematics).

### CS 305: Operating Systems (4 credits)
- **Description**: Process management, multi-threading, concurrency control, synchronization primitives, memory virtualization, virtual memory paging, file systems, and I/O management.
- **Prerequisites**: Minimum grade of C in CS 201 and CS 202.

### CS 310: Database Management Systems (3 credits)
- **Description**: Relational database design, relational algebra, SQL, indexing, transaction processing, ACID properties, normalization forms (1NF through BCNF), and NoSQL architectures.
- **Prerequisites**: Minimum grade of C in CS 201.

### CS 350: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence (3 credits)
- **Description**: State-space search, informed search (A*), constraint satisfaction, game playing (minimax, alpha-beta pruning), probabilistic reasoning, and introduction to supervised learning.
- **Prerequisites**: Minimum grade of C in CS 301 and MATH 260 (Linear Algebra and Probability).

### CS 401: Senior Capstone Design Project I (3 credits)
- **Description**: Team-based software engineering project requiring requirements gathering, architectural specification, prototyping, and agile project management.
- **Prerequisites**: Senior standing (minimum 90 earned credit hours), CS 301, and CS 305.

### CS 402: Senior Capstone Design Project II (3 credits)
- **Description**: Implementation, verification, performance profiling, deployment, and final defense of the capstone project.
- **Prerequisites**: Minimum grade of C in CS 401. Must be taken in the immediate semester following CS 401.

## 3. Academic Standing, Probation, and Suspension Policies

### 3.1 Good Standing
A student is in **Good Academic Standing** if both their cumulative GPA and major GPA remain at or above **2.00**.

### 3.2 Academic Warning
A student whose term GPA drops below **2.00** in any single semester, even if their cumulative GPA remains 2.00 or above, receives an official **Academic Warning**. The student must meet with their academic advisor prior to registering for the next semester.

### 3.3 Academic Probation
- A student is placed on **Academic Probation** when their cumulative GPA falls below **2.00** at the end of any regular semester (Fall or Spring).
- While on probation, a student is restricted to enrolling in a maximum of **13 credit hours** per semester and is ineligible to participate in official university sports or student government executive boards.
- A student on probation has **two consecutive regular semesters** to raise their cumulative GPA to 2.00 or above.

### 3.4 Academic Suspension
- A student who fails to raise their cumulative GPA to at least **2.00** after two consecutive semesters on probation will be placed on **Academic Suspension** for a duration of one full academic year (two regular semesters).
- Re-admission following suspension is not automatic and requires an approved appeal through the University Academic Review Board.

### 3.5 Dean's Honor Roll (Dean's List)
Undergraduate students who complete at least **12 graded semester credits** (excluding Pass/Fail courses) in a regular semester with a term GPA of **3.50 or higher** and no grades of Incomplete (I) or Fail (F) qualify for the **Dean's Honor Roll**.

## 4. Course Registration, Add/Drop, and Withdrawal Regulations

### 4.1 Course Add/Drop Period
- **Regular Add/Drop Deadline**: Students may freely add, swap, or drop courses without academic record or fee penalty through the **10th calendar day** of instruction.
- Dropped courses during this period do not appear on the student's official academic transcript.

### 4.2 Course Withdrawal (W Grade)
- After the 10th instructional day and up until the end of the **10th week** of classes, a student may withdraw from a course by submitting a Course Withdrawal Form.
- A grade of **W** will be recorded on the official transcript. A grade of W does not affect the calculation of the cumulative GPA, but it counts as attempted credits against the Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP) completion rate.
- Students may withdraw from a maximum of **16 credit hours** throughout their undergraduate degree program.

### 4.3 Incomplete Grades (I)
- An Incomplete grade (`I`) may be granted at the discretion of the instructor only when an unforeseen emergency prevents a student who is currently passing the course with at least 70% of coursework completed from finishing the final assignments or exams.
- All outstanding coursework must be submitted no later than the end of the **6th week** of the following regular semester; otherwise, the `I` grade automatically converts to an `F`.

---

## 5. Course Retake and Grade Replacement Policy

- A student may repeat a course in which they earned a grade of **C- or lower**.
- A single course may be repeated for grade replacement a maximum of **two times** (three total attempts).
- Only the **highest grade earned** in the repeated attempts will be factored into the cumulative GPA calculation, although all attempts remain visible on the official transcript.
- Students must submit a Grade Replacement Petition to the Registrar's Office before the end of the term in which the course is retaken.
